# Finnish police passport photo requirements

Summary of *Poliisin passikuvaohje* (Finnish Police passport photo guide, 2020, 7 pages,
available at poliisi.fi). The same rules apply to passports, ID cards and permits, for all
ages. The numbered pictures (e.g. "pic 31") refer to the example photos in the original guide.

## 1. File format (electronic delivery)

- Colour or black-and-white.
- **Exactly 500 × 653 px.** The guide says "not a single pixel of deviation is accepted".
- JPEG (not JPEG2000), extension `.jpg` or `.jpeg`.
- **At most 250 kB.**

## 2. Technical quality

- The photo may be **at most 6 months old**.
- **No editing that changes appearance**, not even small details, and nothing that could
  cast doubt on authenticity. Digital beautification is forbidden. Cropping, scaling,
  levelling and colour-profile conversion are fine. Retouching skin, removing blemishes,
  whitening the background, or changing hair is not.
- Sharp across the whole face: depth of field from the ears to the tip of the nose. It must
  not be blurry, grainy, or show JPEG artefacts (pics 4–6).
- No colour casts (pic 7).
- No optical distortion. Phone cameras are wide-angle and are often used too close, so the
  nose and central features look too large (pic 9). Shoot from about 1.5 m or more, or use
  the 2× lens.

## 3. Dimensions and positioning (pixel values for electronic photos)

Head size is measured **from the crown to the chin tip. Hair and beard do not count**, so
the crown under the hair has to be estimated.

| Measure | Allowed | Paper photo |
|---|---|---|
| Crown → chin | 445–500 px | 32–36 mm |
| Space above the crown | 56–84 px | 4–6 mm |
| Space below the chin | 96–124 px | 7–9 mm |
| Face midline vs image midline | ≤ 21 px | ≤ 1.5 mm |

- There is no separate requirement for head width.
- Hair and beard do not have to be fully visible, though it is recommended.
- Rejected examples: head too high (pic 11), off-centre (pic 12), too big (pic 13), too small (pic 14).

## 4. Pose

- The head is straight: not tilted sideways, forwards or backwards. The face and gaze point
  straight at the camera. Pose is judged from the outline of the head, not from the eyes.
- The shoulders are square to the camera. No over-the-shoulder portrait poses (pic 16).
- Both ears do not need to be equally visible.
- Exceptions are possible for medical reasons.

## 5. Lighting

- Even across the whole face. No shadows on the face or the background, and no blown-out
  highlights from hard light (pics 21–22).
- No red-eye.
- Natural colour, not bluish or reddish.
- Not over- or under-exposed (pics 24–25).

## 6. Expression, glasses, headwear, make-up

- The whole face must be visible, **especially the eyes**.
- Neutral expression. **Mouth closed**. Small babies may have the mouth slightly open.
- **Eyes open**, not squinting, even for small children.
- **Hair or accessories must not cover the face or eyes** (pic 31: hair over the eye is
  rejected). A fringe that reaches the eyelids is a common rejection reason.
- Glasses: frames must not come near the eyes, and there must be no reflections (pics 29–30).
  Taking glasses off is always allowed. Dark glasses or an eye patch are allowed only for
  health reasons.
- A wig is allowed if worn daily. The same rules apply as for hair.
- Headwear is allowed only for religious, customary or medical reasons. It must not cover
  the face or cast shadows on it.
- Make-up is allowed if it does not reduce recognisability.

## 7. Background

- The best background is a **light grey**. What matters most is that the face, hair and
  clothes stand out from it. The laser-engraved image on the card may be greyscale, so a
  light shirt against a light background disappears.
- Plain, even, a single colour, light and neutral.
- No shadows.
- No other people or objects. A small child may be held, but no part of the helper may
  be visible (pics 38–39).
- The background must not be lit so strongly that the outline of the person glows (pic 36).
